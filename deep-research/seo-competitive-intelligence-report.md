# Leon Coe's SEO battlefield: a comprehensive competitive intelligence report

Leon Coe's three companies and personal brand face a **stark visibility gap** in organic search. Of 11 Houston-focused AI keywords tested, amplifyintelligence.ai appeared in **zero** SERPs, amplifiedexec.com **does not appear to exist** as a live site, and leoncoe.com ranked for **none** of the target commercial keywords. The bright spot: houstonaiclub.com ranks **#1** for its branded term and appears in several workshop/community queries. Meanwhile, the "Amplified AI" brand name faces a near-impossible SEO challenge with **6+ competing entities** already dominating that namespace. The opportunity, however, is enormous — Houston's AI market is exploding with **$450M+ in Nvidia/Foxconn investment**, energy sector AI spending projected to triple to **$13.4B by 2029**, and emerging keywords like "fractional AI officer" and "agentic AI consulting" showing thin, conquerable SERPs. This report maps every keyword, competitor, and opportunity across the landscape Leon must navigate.

## Universities form an impenetrable wall in executive AI training SERPs

For "executive AI training," "executive AI program," and "AI leadership program for executives," **universities hold 7-10 of 10 positions** on every SERP tested. Harvard (HBS, HKS, HBS Online, DCE), MIT Sloan, Stanford (GSB, HAI), Wharton, UC Berkeley, Kellogg, Columbia, and Cornell dominate completely. Zero independent consultants or boutique operators rank for these core terms.

University program pages follow a consistent SEO template: **2,000-4,000 words** with structured sections (Overview → Key Benefits → Who Should Attend → Curriculum → Faculty → Pricing → Testimonials → FAQ). Prices range from **$850** (Wharton self-paced) to **$17,500** (Stanford GSB in-person). Most transparently display pricing, include faculty bios with research credentials, and offer brochure downloads gated behind email capture. Notably, **schema markup is weak across the board** — most use basic Organization markup, not Course schema, creating a technical SEO gap any challenger could exploit.

The only non-university content that breaks through consists of: aggregator platforms (edX, Coursera, Emeritus), comparison/listicle sites (executivecourses.com, DigitalDefynd, Poets&Quants), and one rare training company (Oxford Management Centre). For "best executive AI training programs," comparison content earns 3-4 of the top 10 positions — the **highest-ROI content opportunity** for amplifiedexec.com.

### The gap amplifiedexec.com must exploit

The current status of amplifiedexec.com is **pre-launch or non-existent** — zero search results, zero backlinks, zero indexed pages. This is both a problem (no head start) and an opportunity (blank canvas). The exploitable gaps are:

- **Houston/Texas-specific executive AI training**: No competitor targets "executive AI training Houston." Zero results. Wide open.
- **Industry-specific AI training**: University programs are universally industry-agnostic. "AI for energy executives" has zero competition, and Houston is the global energy capital.
- **Small-cohort boutique programs**: Universities serve 40-100+ participants. A **5-15 person** intensive with 1:1 coaching and a custom AI roadmap justifies premium pricing without university overhead.
- **1-2 day intensive format**: Most university programs run 4-8 weeks online or 5-10 days on campus. A "2-Day AI Strategy Sprint" targets busy C-suite executives who can't commit weeks.
- **Board of Directors AI training**: No university explicitly targets board members. Growing regulatory demand (SEC AI disclosure, EU AI Act) creates urgency.
- **Price gap**: The **$3,000-$5,000 range** for a 1-2 day intensive is unoccupied — more practical than MOOCs, more affordable than Stanford, more personal than either.

## LLM citation and generative engine optimization demand a new playbook

How AI assistants recommend local services is becoming as important as traditional search ranking. Key findings from citation research:

**86% of AI citations come from brand-controlled sources** (first-party websites and business listings), not forums. Perplexity averages 21.87 citations per response while ChatGPT uses 7.92. The #1 factor influencing AI citations is **multi-source consensus** — AI systems look for agreement across independent sources (website, Reddit, YouTube, review platforms, industry publications) before recommending a business. Content freshness matters enormously: six-month-old content loses **80% of its citation potential**.

For local professional service queries, AI systems cite business websites with dedicated city/service pages (42.8% citation rate), directory and comparison sites, Forbes rankings, genuine Reddit discussions, YouTube videos, and Google Business Profile data. Prompts with local intent trigger web search in **59% of ChatGPT instances** (versus 31% overall), making well-structured local content critical.

The practical implication: Leon must build **multi-platform consensus** around his brand. This means active YouTube presence, genuine Reddit participation, LinkedIn content, review platform presence (G2, Clutch, Trustpilot), guest features in publications, and comprehensive Google Business Profiles — all telling a consistent brand story. Content should be structured for AI extraction: lead with direct answers in 50-70 words, use clear headings and bullet points, break content into ~800-token chunks, and include original statistics.

## Nonprofit SEO strategy for Houston AI Club should leverage .org and Event schema

Nonprofit status doesn't provide a direct ranking advantage, but it unlocks significant indirect benefits. The most valuable is **Google Ad Grants** — up to **$10,000/month in free Google Ads**, which could drive substantial traffic to houstonaiclub.com (or a future houstonaiclub.org) for workshop and community keywords.

A **.org domain is recommended** for Houston AI Club. It signals mission-driven, community-focused identity (83% consumer awareness), differentiates from for-profit AI companies, and aligns with the nonprofit's actual status. The domain houstonaiclub.org would carry stronger trust signals for donors, volunteers, and institutional partners.

The most critical schema markup for Houston AI Club is:

- **NGO schema** with the `nonprofitStatus` property set to the appropriate USNonprofitType (e.g., `Nonprofit501c3`)
- **Event schema** on every event page — this enables rich event snippets in search results and is essential for an event-based organization
- **FAQPage schema** for community questions about AI learning, joining, and events
- **Person schema** for Leon Coe linking founder identity to the organization
- **VideoObject schema** for any recorded workshops or talks

Currently, **none of Leon's properties have any detectable structured data** — no Organization, LocalBusiness, Event, Service, FAQ, or Person schema. Implementing comprehensive schema markup across all four properties is a high-impact, low-effort win.

The data points to a clear action hierarchy. First, **establish basic infrastructure**: create Google Business Profiles for Amplify Intelligence and Houston AI Club, implement schema markup across all properties, and migrate Substack content to owned domains. Second, **claim the unclaimed**: build "Houston's AI Guy" as an ownable keyword through consistent cross-platform usage, pursue a Google Knowledge Panel through Wikipedia-eligible press coverage, and create dedicated landing pages for every target keyword. Fourth, **exploit competitors' gaps**: Opinosis isn't actually in Houston, Octaria's AI page is thin, Trenegy lacks social proof on its AI page, and houston-ai.org has almost no content. Fifth, **build multi-platform consensus** for LLM citation: YouTube channel, review profiles, directory listings, Reddit presence, and guest publications — all reinforcing a consistent brand narrative.

The "Amplified AI" brand name requires a strategic decision: either invest heavily in disambiguation (long-tail branded terms, modifier-rich naming) or consider whether a less contested brand name would better serve the business's SEO trajectory over the next decade. With 6+ established competitors in the namespace and a NASDAQ-backed company actively marketing a product called "Amplified AI," the cost of competing for brand-term visibility is exceptionally high.

The fundamental competitive advantage Leon holds is **authenticity of local presence**. He is a lifelong Houstonian running the city's largest AI community, with 2,500+ members and 130-226 attendees at monthly events. His strongest competitor for Houston AI consulting terms (Opinosis Analytics) is based in Utah. The SEO strategy should be built entirely around this authenticity advantage — transforming real-world community leadership into digital authority through content, structured data, backlinks, and multi-platform presence.
